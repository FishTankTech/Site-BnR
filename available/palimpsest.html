Synopsis

In 1996, Robert Cremean wrote The Tenth Arch, A Sequel to Vatican Corridor, A Non-Specific Autobiography in which he first used the concept of the palimpsest saying:

"We define our repetitive behavior episodically, creating beginnings and endings an infinitely layered palimpsest that denies process and creates the illusion of linearity. Life beyond death. Escape. Reward. A plant worth more than the sum of its seeds. Ego. Disconnection. And within the book are dialogues and parables which he named A Palimpsest in Five Erasures."

When asked what he meant by palimpsest, he spoke of the ancient Egyptian manuscripts which were written on papyrus, the writings erased and written over, erased and written over again, each erasure leaving traces of itself even when over-written.

On it are recalled many of those past and ever-present ideas, themes, images and symbols, layer upon layer, conceived and over-written, conceived and overwritten again. Perhaps its very form denies the concept of linearity as if time itself has no beginning and no end and wherein ideas and images are endlessly conceived and reconceived, a sculpture around which one may walk endlessly, each view of it a new beginning.
