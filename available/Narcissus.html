Synopsis

The Greek myth of Narcissus has been written about since first penned. He was pictured as a beautiful youth at one time heartbroken at the death of his (most often) male lover and at others by the death of his female lover. Culture-makers have always thrived on adapting almost everything to their own cause or metaphor, including by our most noted formative psychiatrists and by countless others making "narcissism" an everyday clinical and cultural cliché. Perhaps Narcissus is best known for having seen his image in still waters, that he became so hopelessly enamored of himself that he stared at his image until his death.

Here, The myth is again revisited. But in this retelling, it is his reflection in the pond that speaks. "All the metaphors have changed." Mirroring man-kind, Narcissus seems unaware of that fact.
